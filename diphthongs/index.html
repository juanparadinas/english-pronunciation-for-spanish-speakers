    Diphthongs (8 sounds)¶

    A diphthong is one vowel that glides into another inside a single syllable. English has 8. Spanish has diphthongs too, but the English ones differ in quality and in the glide, and the British centring diphthongs (ending in schwa) do not exist in Spanish.

    1. What they are¶

    Eight gliding vowels. Three groups:

    • Closing to /ɪ/: /eɪ/, /aɪ/, /ɔɪ/
    • Closing to /ʊ/: /aʊ/, /əʊ/
    • Centring to /ə/ (British, from a dropped R): /ɪə/, /eə/, /ʊə/

    Spanish contrast: the centring diphthongs (/ɪə/, /eə/, /ʊə/) are the hard ones. They exist in SSBE because the final R is not pronounced and becomes a schwa. Spanish speakers either pronounce the R or use a pure vowel.

    2. How to produce them¶

    Start on the first vowel and glide smoothly toward the second; do not break it into two syllables. For centring diphthongs, glide into a relaxed schwa and do not pronounce the R (British).

    Diphthong As in Typical spellings Examples

    4. Angle 2 — spellings per diphthong (fuller lists)¶

    • /eɪ/: day, stay, play, make, rate, late, great, eight, able, brain, straight, nail, pain, they, grey, veil.
    • /aɪ/: try, life, like, mind, find, night, right, buy, my, high, light, sky, die, lie.
    • /ɔɪ/: toy, boy, annoy, noise, join, voice, point, enjoy, coin, oil.
    • /aʊ/: now, how, house, round, out, sound, loud, mouth, down, town, cloud, announce, doubt, around, south, count.
    • /əʊ/: home, go, so, low, slow, don't, road, boat, show, though, although, shoulder, cold, most.
    • /ɪə/: here, ear, tear (drop), cheer, peer, steer, dear, deer, beer, fierce, idea, serious.
    • /eə/: there, where, care, stare, air, chair, hair, their, bear, area, parents, square.
    • /ʊə/: tour, cure, pure, mature, during, usually, sure, poor, Europe, jury.

    5. Minimal pairs / contrasts¶

    Pair Contrast
    beer / bear /ɪə/ vs /eə/
    here / hair /ɪə/ vs /eə/
    go / saw /əʊ/ vs /ɔː/
    home / ham /əʊ/ vs /æ/
    by / bay /aɪ/ vs /eɪ/
    so / sew(n) vs saw /əʊ/ vs /ɔː/

    6. Common Spanish error¶

    The trap 1: pronouncing the written R in centring diphthongs (here, care, tour), which breaks the British sound. The trap 2: /əʊ/ said as a pure Spanish "o" (go = "gou" with Spanish o). The fix: glide into a schwa, no R (British). For /əʊ/, start from schwa, not from "o" (this is the main UK/US difference too: US uses /oʊ/).
