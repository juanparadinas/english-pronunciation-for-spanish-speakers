    More spelling rules (and negative rules)¶

    Additional reliable patterns, plus "negative rules" (what a letter never does). Negative rules are high-value for Spanish speakers because they eliminate a whole class of errors at once.

    Negative rules (what never happens)¶

    A written "c" is NEVER /z/¶

    English "c" is only: - hard /k/ before a, o, u, or a consonant: cat, cot, cut, clock, cream. - soft /s/ before e, i, y: city, cent, cycle, precise, decide, receive.

    It is never /z/ and never /θ/ in standard English. So "precise", "decision", "city" have /s/. A common Spanish pitfall is voicing it; do not.

    A stressed written "a" is NEVER /ʌ/¶

    A written "a" in a stressed syllable is never the /ʌ/ of "cut". It is usually the front /æ/ (cat), and can also be /eɪ/ (cake), /ɑː/ (car, past), /ɔː/ (call, water), /ɒ/ (want, wash), or /eə/ (care) — but never /ʌ/. The /ʌ/ sound is spelled u, o, or ou (cut, love, touch), not a. So never read "cat" as "cut".

    Other quick negatives¶

    • A single written "s" at the start of a word is /s/, never /z/ (so, sun, send). Initial /z/ is spelled z (zoo).
    • "ss" is /s/ or /ʃ/, never /z/ (except dessert, scissors, possess, dissolve, hussar). See double consonants.
    • "ph" is /f/, never /p/+/h/.

    Vowel teams are almost never read literally¶

    Two written vowels (a vowel team) are almost never pronounced as the two Spanish vowels in sequence. ai is not "a-i", ea is not "e-a", oa is not "o-a". A team usually spells ONE sound, either a long vowel or a diphthong.

    Teams that resemble their spelling (the partial exceptions)¶

    These glide diphthongs do roughly track the letters, because the sound glides from the first vowel toward the second:

    Team Sound Examples
    ay, ai, ey /eɪ/ day, rain, survey, they, obey
    oy, oi /ɔɪ/ boy, boil, enjoy
    ou, ow /aʊ/ house, now, loud

    The /əʊ/ trap (British vs American)¶

    The go/home vowel is NOT "o-u". In British English it is /əʊ/ = schwa + ʊ (go, home, though, road, show). Americans use /oʊ/, which is closer to the spelling. Spanish speakers must not read it as a Spanish "o" or "ou".

    Second value: unstressed ey/ay -> /i/¶

    When unstressed and final, ey/ay weaken to /i/ (not /eɪ/): money, honey, monkey, journey, Sunday, Monday, holiday.

    EI — the showcase irregular team¶

    "ei" is the clearest proof that teams are not read literally. It has at least five values and no reliable rule; each word is learned:

    Sound Examples Note
    /eɪ/ vein, veil, rein, reign, feign, eight, weight, neighbour, sleigh, freight the "they" vowel
    /iː/ receive, deceive, conceive, perceive, ceiling, seize, protein, caffeine long i; surprises Spanish speakers; "i before e except after c"
    /aɪ/ height, sleight, Einstein, feisty, heist; either/neither (UK) the "my" diphthong
    /e/ heifer, leisure (UK), Leicester short e
    /ə/ or /ɪ/ foreign, forfeit, surfeit, counterfeit, sovereign weak, unstressed

    Note the spelling distinction: "ey" (survey, they, money) and "ei" (vein, receive, height) behave differently; do not conflate them. The biggest trap in "ei" is the long /iː/ group (receive, ceiling, protein), which looks nothing like its sound.

    The letter X¶

    Written "x" has three values; the first two are rule-governed.

    Default: /ks/¶

    Most of the time x is /ks/: box, tax, relax, fix, six, exit (variant), expect, extra, exercise, excellent, expert, complex, text, next.

    /gz/ — before a stressed vowel-initial syllable¶

    X becomes /gz/ (the voiced counterpart) when it sits immediately before a stressed syllable that begins with a vowel sound:

    exámple, exáct, exám, exémption, exíst, exháust (h is silent -> vowel), exótic, exággerate, exécutive, exémplary, exúberant.

    Two things block /gz/ (so it stays /ks/)¶

    1. Stress is NOT on the following syllable. If the stress is before the x, it stays /ks/: éxercise, éxcellent, éxpert, éxhale (variant), éxecute.
    2. The following sound is a consonant, not a vowel. The syllable after x then starts with a consonant, so /gz/ cannot apply:
    3. exc- (c = /s/): exception, excite, excel, exceed, excellent. "exc-" is always /ks/ even when stress follows (excéption), because of the c.
    4. exp-: expect, explain, express, expand.
    5. ext-: extend, extreme, extract.
    6. excl-/expl-: exclude, explode.

    So exception is /ɪkˈsep.../ (ks) while example is /ɪgˈzɑːm.../ (gz): both have the stress after the x, but exception's next sound is the consonant /s/.

    Word-initial x -> /z/¶

    At the start of a word, x is /z/, never /ks/: xylophone, xerox, xenon, xenophobia, Xena.

    Edge cases¶

    • exit: /ˈeksɪt/ or /ˈegzɪt/ (both heard).
    • luxury /ˈlʌkʃəri/ (ks -> kʃ) vs luxurious /lʌgˈʒʊəriəs/ (gz -> gʒ): same stress-driven voicing, palatalised before the yod.
    • Final -x and most clusters stay /ks/: six, box, complexion, anxious (/ŋkʃ/).

    Soft vs hard C and G¶

    Letter Before e, i, y Before a, o, u / consonant
    c soft /s/: city, cent, cycle hard /k/: cat, cot, cut, clip
    g soft /dʒ/ (usually): gym, giant, gentle hard /g/: go, gap, gun, glad

    G is less regular than c: some words keep hard /g/ before e/i (get, give, girl, gift, begin, tiger). C is highly regular.

    -AL tends to /ɔːl/¶

    Before a consonant and word-finally, "al" often becomes /ɔːl/ (the l is a dark l): call, tall, wall, fall, hall, ball, small, always, also, although, salt, halt, false, bald, walk, talk, chalk (these last three with silent l -> /ɔːk/).

    But this is a tendency, not a law. Exceptions keep /æl/: shall, pal, alley, valley, balance, talent, salad, canal (final stress), Alabama. And "al" + vowel is usually /æl/ (alien is /eɪ/; value /æl/).

    Takeaway: "al + consonant" leans strongly to /ɔːl/; "al + vowel" usually stays /æl/.

    Intervocalic a + r -> /eə/ (vary, Mary, parent)¶

    When a stressed "a" is followed by a single r plus another vowel, it is the centring /eə/ (UK adds a mini schwa before the r), not /æ/ or /ɑː/:

    vary, various, variable, Mary, scary, canary, aquarium, parent, area, rarity, Sarah, wary, library (first a).

    Contrast with a doubled rr, which closes the syllable and gives short /æ/: carry, marry, Harry, narrow. So vary /ˈveəri/ vs carry /ˈkæri/. See double consonants.

    The same single-r-before-vowel pattern gives /ɪə/ for "e/i" (hero, serious, Vera, query, period) and /ʊə/ / /jʊə/ for "u" (fury, curious, Europe).

    -OUGH (the seven-way spelling)¶

    "ough" is the most irregular string in English:

    Sound Examples
    /ɔː/ thought, bought, ought, sought, nought
    /əʊ/ though, dough, although
    /uː/ through
    /ʌf/ enough, rough, tough
    /ɒf/ cough, trough
    /aʊ/ bough, plough, drought
    /ə/ borough, thorough (UK)

    No rule; each must be learned. Listed here so the trap is visible.

    Y as a vowel¶

    • Stressed, final or alone: /aɪ/ — my, cry, by, try, type, cycle.
    • Unstressed, final: /i/ (close to /ɪ/) — happy, city, funny, quickly.
    • Medial: /ɪ/ — gym, system, myth, crystal.

    GH¶

    • Silent: light, night, though, through, daughter, high.
    • /f/: enough, rough, tough, cough, laugh (the -ough/-augh set).
    • Never a hard /g/+/h/.

    Common Spanish error¶

    The trap: voicing c to /z/, reading stressed "a" as /ʌ/, or reading "al" as a plain /al/. The fix: internalise the negatives (c never /z/; stressed a never /ʌ/) and the al -> /ɔːl/ tendency. They remove whole error classes.
