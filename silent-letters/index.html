    Silent letters¶

    English is full of written letters that are not pronounced. They follow recognisable patterns. Learning the patterns stops Spanish speakers from "spelling-pronouncing" words like doubt, know, should, or hour.

    Source: "The phonemes of British English and their spelling" (archived). Organised most-common-first: the top patterns are the ones worth learning today; the rarer ones are further down.

    Quick map¶

    Letter Silent in (main pattern) Flagship examples
    l -alk, -alm, -alf, -ould, -olk walk, calm, half, would, folk
    b -mb, -bt climb, thumb, debt, doubt
    k kn- know, knife, knee
    w wr-, who-, -wick write, who, Greenwich
    h wh-, h-, -ah what, hour, honest
    t -stle, -sten, French -et castle, listen, ballet
    n -mn autumn, column, hymn
    g gn-, -gn, -gm gnome, sign, design
    gh -ight, -ough light, though
    p ps-, pn-, pt- psychology, pneumonia
    c -sc- muscle, scissors

    Most common¶

    Silent L — before k, m, f, and in -ould¶

    This is the highest-value one; it is very regular.

    • -alk -> /ɔːk/: walk, talk, chalk, stalk.
    • -alm -> /ɑːm/: calm, palm, balm, psalm, qualm.
    • -alf / -alv -> /ɑːf/, /ɑːv/: half, calf, behalf.
    • -ould: could, should, would.
    • -olk / -olm: folk, yolk, Holmes, Suffolk, Norfolk.
    • also: salmon, almond, Malcolm, Lincoln.

    Note: the l is pronounced in many other al words (also, always, salt, bald, false). The silent-l set above is specific; learn it as a list.

    Silent B — after m (-mb) and before t (-bt)¶

    • -mb -> /m/: climb, comb, crumb, numb, thumb, tomb, womb, lamb, limb, bomb, dumb, plumber.
    • -bt -> /t/: debt, doubt, subtle, subtly.
    • Restored before a vowel suffix: bomb -> bombard.
    • Keeps /b/ (not this pattern): tumble, humble, nimble, assemble.

    Silent K — initial kn-¶

    Always silent before n at the start: know, knowledge, knife, knight, knee, kneel, knock, knot, knit, knob, knuckle.

    Silent W — wr-, who-, and -wick/-wich names¶

    • wr- -> /r/: write, wrist, wrong, wrap, wreck, wrestle, wring, wriggle.
    • who-words: who, whom, whose, whole, whore.
    • -wich / -wick place names: Greenwich, Norwich, Warwick, Chiswick.
    • also: answer, sword, two.

    Silent H — initial and wh-¶

    • Silent initial h: hour, honest, honour, heir, vehicle, vehement, annihilate; and words from French.
    • wh- where the w carries the sound (h silent): what, when, where, which, why, while, white, wheel. (These are /w/ in SSBE.)
    • wh- where the h carries the sound (w silent): who, whose, whole.
    • Final h is silent: cheetah, hurrah, oh, pharaoh, shah, verandah.
    • -ham place names: Durham, Nottingham, Chatham, Birmingham.

    Fairly common¶

    Silent T — -stle, -sten, and French -et/-ot¶

    • -stle -> /sl/: castle, whistle, thistle, bustle, rustle, wrestle, jostle.
    • -sten -> /sn/: listen, fasten, glisten, hasten, moisten, christen.
    • French -et/-ot (final t silent): ballet, beret, buffet, gourmet, cabaret, debut, depot, rapport, ricochet.
    • also: Christmas, mortgage; often (t optional).

    Silent N — final -mn¶

    Silent after m at the end of a word: autumn, column, damn, condemn, hymn, solemn. Restored before a vowel suffix: autumnal, columnist, condemnation, hymnal, solemnity.

    Silent G — gn- / -gn / -gm¶

    • Initial gn- -> /n/: gnome, gnaw, gnat, gnarl, gnostic.
    • Final -gn -> /n/: sign, design, resign, align, foreign, reign, campaign, champagne, feign, benign, malign, assign, consign.
    • Final -gm -> /m/: diaphragm, paradigm, phlegm.
    • Restored before a vowel suffix: sign -> signal, resign -> resignation, paradigm -> paradigmatic, phlegm -> phlegmatic.

    GH — silent or /f/¶

    • Silent: light, night, right, sight, high, though, through, bought, daughter, weigh, eight, straight.
    • /f/: enough, rough, tough, cough, laugh, draught.
    • Never a hard /g/+/h/.

    Less common / learn as needed¶

    Silent P — Greek ps-, pn-, pt-¶

    psychology, psalm, pseudo, pneumonia, pneumatic, pterodactyl; also corps, coup, receipt, cupboard, raspberry.

    Silent C — -sc-¶

    muscle, scissors, sceptre, scene, science, descend, ascent, corpuscle, fascinate; also indict, victuals (c silent).

    Silent others¶

    • ch: fuchsia /ˈfjuːʃə/, yacht /jɒt/.
    • s: island, aisle, isle, debris, Illinois.
    • u (after g): guard, guess, guitar, guide, league, tongue, vogue.
    • others: Wednesday (d), handkerchief (d), sandwich (d), colonel (first l).

    The rule to remember¶

    Many silent letters come back when a vowel suffix is added, because the letter then starts a new syllable: autumn -> autumnal, sign -> signal, bomb -> bombard, condemn -> condemnation. If you can hear it in a relative of the word, it is still "there" in the spelling.

    Common Spanish error¶

    The trap: pronouncing every written letter (the b in doubt, the k in know, the l in should, the h in hour, the p in psychology). The fix: learn the top patterns (silent l in walk/calm/would; silent b in climb/debt; silent k in know; silent w in write; silent h in hour). They are regular enough to trust.
