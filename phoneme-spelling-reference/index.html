    Phoneme-spelling reference (SSBE)¶

    An authoritative, exhaustive mapping of every British English phoneme to all the ways it can be spelled, including one-off spellings, silent letters, and the sound alternations that mark grammar. This page summarises the source and points to where its detail lives in the wiki.

    Source: "The phonemes of British English and their spelling" (9 pages, archived in archive/). It is organised Angle-2 style (sound -> spellings), the exact complement of the wiki's per-sound pages. This page leads with the common spellings for everyday use; rare and one-off spellings are kept in a labelled appendix at the bottom.

    What the source covers¶

    • Every vowel, diphthong, and consonant phoneme with its full spelling set.
    • One-off / rare spellings per phoneme (e.g. /iː/ from "oe" in foetus, "ae" in Caesar, "is" in debris).
    • Silent letters, by letter and rule (see silent letters).
    • Grammar-marking sound alternations (below).
    • A detailed yod (/j/) distribution (below).

    Common spellings per phoneme (quick reference)¶

    Use this as the day-to-day lookup: the high-frequency spelling(s) for each sound. Rare and one-off spellings are in the appendix at the bottom, not here.

    Unique additions folded into the wiki¶

    /θ/ - /ð/ alternation (noun vs verb)¶

    Spelling signals it: nouns end -th (voiceless /θ/), verbs end -the (voiced /ð/).

    Noun /θ/ Verb /ð/
    bath bathe
    breath breathe
    sheath sheathe
    teeth teethe
    wreath wreathe

    Also north/northern, south/southern, worth/worthy use /θ/ -> /ð/. Position rules: initial th in function words is /ð/ (the, this, then), but content words are /θ/ (think, through); medial th + -er/-ing is usually /ð/ (other, bathing) with exceptions (panther, ether, anything). See /θ/ and /ð/.

    /s/ - /z/ alternation (noun vs verb)¶

    Final -se can be /s/ or /z/; some pairs are spelled to mark it:

    Noun /s/ Verb /z/
    advice advise
    device devise
    licence (UK noun) license (UK verb)
    practice (UK noun) practise (UK verb)

    Invisible in spelling: abuse, close, diffuse, excuse, house, use (noun /s/, verb /z/). Fixed: decrease, increase, promise, release always /s/; fuse, surprise always /z/. See /s/ vs /z/.

    Yod (/j/) distribution¶

    /j/ appears before /uː/ and /ʊə/ when spelled u, ue, eu, ew, ure, and NOT preceded by j, ch, r, or consonant+l.

    • With yod: tube, duty, music, fuel, due, cue, Tuesday, feud, neutral, ewe, few, new, mural, euro, pure, mature.
    • Without yod (dropped): Jew, June, juice, chew, chute, rude, crew, prune, blue, plural, sluice.

    The two-syllable /ɪə/ often compresses to /jə/ (audience, idiot, India); /uə/ to /wə/ (usual, innocuous). See the letter U and long u /uː/.

    How to use this page¶

    For "how is this word spelled for sound X", come here or to the relevant per-sound page (Angle 2 sections). For "how is this spelling pronounced", use the letter pages (A, U, OU) and spelling-to-sound rules.
