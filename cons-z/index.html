    Consonant: /z/¶

    Does not exist in Spanish. Spanish s is always voiceless, so speakers miss the many English /z/ sounds, including most plural and verb endings.

    1. What it is¶

    /z/ is a voiced alveolar fricative: like /s/ but with voice (vocal cords buzzing). The sound in "zoo", "is", "please".

    Spanish contrast: Spanish has no /z/; the letter s is always /s/. Spanish speakers devoice /z/ to /s/, so "is" becomes "iss" and "zoo" becomes "soo".

    2. How to produce it¶

    Same tongue position as /s/ (tip near the alveolar ridge) but turn on the voice so it buzzes. It is the voiced partner of /s/.

    3. Angle 1 — when a spelling makes /z/¶

    Two spellings dominate: the letter z, and (very commonly) a single s between vowels. The letter z is almost always /z/.

    Spelling Examples
    z (almost always /z/) zoo, zero, zebra, size, prize, lazy, dozen
    s between vowels easy, music, present, reason, visit, busy, cousin
    s in "is/was/as/his" is, was, as, his, has
    final s after voiced sound dogs, plays, loves (plural/3rd-person /z/)
    x (= /gz/) exam, exact, example, exist (see letter X rule)
    -se endings please, these, choose, cause, pause

    Note on the letter z: it is /z/ in nearly every word. The rare exception is /ʒ/ in azure and seizure, and even those are often said with /z/. Compare also the single s between vowels, which is the other big /z/ source.

    Note on ss: a doubled ss is NOT /z/; it is voiceless /s/ (pass, miss) or /ʃ/ (mission). Only five ss words take /z/: dessert, scissors, possess, dissolve, hussar. See double consonants.

    4. Angle 2 — where the /z/ sound is written¶

    use, please, expose, tuesday, wednesday, thursday, close, was, lose, exam, exactly, exist, exemption, example, easy, is, as, realise, emphasize, size, advise, musician, cause, mouse, pause, amuse, because, excuse, refuse, monetization, rationalization, specialization, automatization.

    5. Minimal pairs / contrasts¶

    /s/ /z/
    ice eyes
    price prize
    bus buzz
    face phase
    loose lose
    racer razor

    6. Common Spanish error¶

    The trap: devoicing every /z/ to /s/ (is = iss, zoo = soo), and getting plural/verb endings wrong. The fix: add voice. Same mouth as /s/, vocal cords on. Train /s/-/z/ pairs (ice/eyes, price/prize) and the plural rule (dogs = /z/, cats = /s/).

    /s/ - /z/ alternation (noun vs verb)¶

    Final -se varies between /s/ and /z/; some pairs are spelled to mark it: advice/advise, device/devise, and the UK pairs licence/license, practice/practise (noun /s/, verb /z/). Invisible in spelling: abuse, close, diffuse, excuse, house, use (noun /s/, verb /z/).

    Fixed values (do not alternate): decrease, increase, promise, release always /s/; fuse, surprise always /z/.

    Suffix rules (from the source): - -ze always /z/ (maze, seize, organize). - -ese always /iːz/ (Chinese, Portuguese). - -ism always /ɪzəm/ (capitalism, racism). - -ce always /s/ (advice, race); only desert, scissors, possess, dissolve, hussar have /z/ for ss.

    See phoneme-spelling reference.
