# Consonant: /r/

> The English R is nothing like the Spanish R. Getting it wrong marks a Spanish
> accent immediately.

## 1. What it is

/r/ is an approximant: the tongue approaches the roof of the mouth but never
touches or vibrates. The sound in "red", "around", "very".

Spanish contrast: Spanish R is a tap or trill (the tongue taps or vibrates
against the ridge). English R has no contact and no vibration. This is the
single most accent-defining consonant.

Accent note: SSBE is non-rhotic, R is only pronounced before a vowel (red,
very), not before a consonant or at the end (car, hard, mother have no R
sound). American is rhotic and pronounces all of them.

## 2. How to produce it

Curl or bunch the tongue back slightly so the tip points up toward the ridge
but does NOT touch it. Round the lips a little. No tap, no trill. The tongue
is still.

## 3. Angle 1 — when a spelling makes /r/ (British)

| Spelling | Pronounced? | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| r before a vowel | yes | red, around, very, bright, carry |
| r before consonant | no (British) | car(d), hard, park, first |
| r word-final | no (British) | car, mother, far (unless linking to next vowel) |
| rr | yes (one /r/) | carry, marry, mirror |

## 4. Angle 2 — where the /r/ sound is written

Always the letter r or rr: red, run, around, very, bright, carry, marry,
mirror, rural, iron, serenity, natural.

Linking R (British): a final written R is pronounced when the next word starts
with a vowel: "far away", "mother and father".

## 5. Minimal pairs / contrasts

| Pair | Contrast |
|---|---|
| right / light | /r/ vs /l/ |
| road / load | /r/ vs /l/ |
| train / twain | cluster clarity |
| correct (tap vs approximant) | Spanish tap vs English /r/ |

## 6. Common Spanish error

> The trap: tapping or trilling the R as in Spanish (perro/pero).
> The fix: no contact, no vibration. Tongue pulled back, lips slightly
> rounded. In British English, do not pronounce R before consonants or at the
> end of words.
