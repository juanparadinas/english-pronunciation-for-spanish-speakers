# Consonant: /ð/ (voiced TH)

> The voiced "th" in "this". Spanish has the sound only as an allophone, so
> speakers often replace it with /d/.

## 1. What it is

/ð/ is a voiced dental fricative, the "th" in "this", "mother", "breathe".

Spanish contrast: Spanish has a similar sound as the soft "d" between vowels
(nada, cada), but not as a distinct phoneme at the start of words. Spanish
speakers replace initial /ð/ with a hard /d/, so "this" becomes "dis".

## 2. How to produce it

Tongue tip lightly between or against the top teeth, push voiced air with
friction. It is the voiced partner of /θ/. The tongue touches the teeth, unlike
/d/ (ridge) or /z/ (ridge).

## 3. Angle 1 — spellings that make /ð/

Always written th. /ð/ (voiced) appears in:

| Position | Examples |
|---|---|
| function words (initial) | the, this, that, these, those, they, them, then, there |
| between vowels | mother, father, brother, other, weather, together |
| -the endings | breathe, bathe, clothe, smooth |

## 4. Angle 2 — where the /ð/ sound is written

the, this, that, these, those, they, them, then, there, than, though, thus,
mother, father, brother, other, another, weather, rather, together, either,
breathe, bathe, clothe, smooth, with (often).

## 5. Minimal pairs / contrasts

| Pair | Contrast |
|---|---|
| they / day | /ð/ vs /d/ |
| then / den | /ð/ vs /d/ |
| there / dare | /ð/ vs /d/ |
| breathe / breed | /ð/ vs /d/ |
| though / dough | /ð/ vs /d/ |

Also /ð/ vs /z/: with/wizz, breathe/breeze.

## 6. Common Spanish error

> The trap: replacing /ð/ with a hard /d/, so "this" = "dis", "they" = "day".
> The fix: tongue to the teeth, voiced friction. Use the Spanish soft-d sound
> in "nada" as a starting point, then bring it to the front teeth.
