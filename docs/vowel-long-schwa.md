# Vowel: long schwa /ɜː/

> The second most important vowel for Spanish speakers. A long, stressed
> central vowel that almost no Spanish native produces correctly.

## 1. What it is

/ɜː/ is a long, central vowel that always carries stress. Think of it as a
stressed, lengthened cousin of the schwa, held longer and said with intent.
It is the vowel in "bird", "her", "turn".

Spanish contrast: it does not exist in Spanish. Spanish speakers substitute a
full vowel (usually /e/ or /a/) plus a rolled R, which sounds foreign and
often breaks the word.

Note on accent: in SSBE (non-rhotic) the R after it is silent and the vowel is
held long. In American (rhotic) the R is pronounced, so the vowel is shorter
and R-coloured (often written /ɝ/).

## 2. How to produce it

Tongue central and mid, like the schwa, but hold it and give it full voice and
length. Lips neutral, slightly spread. In British English, do not pronounce
the R that follows; let the vowel carry the whole sound.

## 3. Angle 1 — when a spelling makes /ɜː/

The sound is almost always a vowel + R in a stressed syllable.

| Spelling | Examples |
|---|---|
| ir | bird, girl, first, shirt, birth, circle, virgin, confirm |
| ur | turn, purge, curve, further, turtle, burst, disturb |
| er | her, term, server, concern, observe, reverse, emerge, personal |
| ear | learn, earth, heard, search, early |
| wor | world, work, worse, worth, worm (w + or is the exception that gives /ɜː/, not /ɔː/) |

## 4. Angle 2 — where the /ɜː/ sound is written

| Spelling | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ur | purpose, purge, precursor, disturb, burglary, burst, turbulence, further, turn | |
| wor | world, work, worse, worth, worm, worry (US) | NOT "worn" (/ɔː/) |

## 5. Minimal pairs / contrasts

| Pair | Contrast |
|---|---|
| bird / beard | /ɜː/ vs /ɪə/ |
| further / farther | /ɜː/ vs /ɑː/ |
| work / walk | /ɜː/ vs /ɔː/ |
| hurt / heart | /ɜː/ vs /ɑː/ |
| stir / star | /ɜː/ vs /ɑː/ |

## 6. Common Spanish error

> The trap: saying a full Spanish vowel plus a rolled R (e.g. "bird" as
> "berd" with a tapped r).
> The fix: one long central vowel, no R movement in British English. The
> tongue barely moves; the sound is held, not rolled.
