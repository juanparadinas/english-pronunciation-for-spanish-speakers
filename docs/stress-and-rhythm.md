# Stress and rhythm

> English is stress-timed; Spanish is syllable-timed. This single difference
> reshapes the whole rhythm of the language and is why fluent English can sound
> "swallowed" to a Spanish ear.

## 1. Stress-timed vs syllable-timed

- Spanish (syllable-timed): every syllable takes roughly the same time. The
  rhythm is even, like a metronome on each syllable.
- English (stress-timed): stressed syllables fall at roughly regular intervals,
  and the unstressed syllables between them are compressed to fit. The rhythm
  rides on the stressed beats.

Consequence: in English, unstressed syllables shorten and their vowels reduce
to schwa. A Spanish speaker giving every syllable full value sounds robotic and
is hard to follow.

## 2. Word stress

Every multi-syllable word has one main stressed syllable. The stressed syllable
is louder, longer, and higher; the rest tend toward schwa.

Getting word stress wrong is often worse than getting a vowel wrong: "PHOtograph
/ phoTOgraphy / photoGRAPHic" shift stress and change the vowels entirely.

### Noun vs verb stress shift
Many words stress syllable 1 as a noun, syllable 2 as a verb:
PROgress / proGRESS, REcord / reCORD, CONtract / conTRACT. See
[unwritten rules](unwritten-rules.md).

## 3. Sentence stress: content vs function words

At the sentence level the same weakening happens to whole words:

- Content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs) carry stress.
- Function words (articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, pronouns, conjunctions)
  weaken to their weak forms with a schwa.

Example: "I was going to the SHOP to BUY some BREAD." The capitalised content
words land on the beat; everything else compresses (was -> /wəz/, to -> /tə/,
some -> /səm/).

## 4. Weak forms

Common function words and their weak (schwa) forms: was /wəz/, been /bɪn/,
from /frəm/, of /əv/, and /ən/, to /tə/, for /fə/, can /kən/, them /ðəm/,
are /ə/, have /həv/.

See [connected speech](connected-speech.md) for how weak forms combine with
linking and elision.

## 5. Common Spanish error

> The trap: giving every syllable and every word equal weight (syllable-timed
> rhythm), with full vowels throughout.
> The fix: find the content words, hit them on the beat, and compress
> everything between to schwas. Rhythm first, then individual sounds.
