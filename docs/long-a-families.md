# Long /ɑː/ word families (UK TRAP-BATH)

> In SSBE, a large set of words spelled with "a" take the long back /ɑː/ where
> American keeps the short front /æ/. This is the TRAP-BATH split. The words
> group neatly by the consonant(s) that follow the "a".

See [/ɑː/ (back a)](vowel-long-a-back.md) for the sound itself.

## Why group by the following consonant

The split is not random: SSBE lengthened "a" to /ɑː/ mainly before certain
consonant environments, voiceless fricatives (/f/, /θ/, /s/) and some nasal
clusters. Grouping by what follows makes the pattern learnable.

## Families

### a + S sounds (/s/, /ss/, /st/, /sk/, /sp/)
grass, class, glass, pass, brass, last, fast, past, cast, vast, mast, blast,
ask, task, mask, basket, rascal, grasp, clasp, plaster, master, disaster.

### a + TH (/θ/)
bath, path, lath, aftermath. (Not "math" in American; UK says /bɑːθ/.)

### a + F (/f/)
after, craft, draft, graft, shaft, raft, staff, laugh (gh = /f/), daft.

### a + N + consonant (/ns/, /nt/, /ntʃ/, /nd/, /ntʃ/)
dance, chance, France, glance, advance, prance, branch, ranch, command,
demand, grant, plant, slant, answer, example, sample, can't, shan't.

### a + R (always /ɑː/, both UK and US core)
car, far, start, hard, park, large, March, garden, market, argue, guard,
charge, sharp, part, art, card. (This group is /ɑː/ in both accents; R-colour
in American.)

### al with silent l (/ɑː/)
calm, palm, half, calf, almond. (The l is silent; see
[silent letters](silent-letters.md).)

### Misc / foreign
father, drama, pasta (UK), banana (final a), tomato (UK), vase (UK).

## What stays short /æ/ (does NOT join the split)

Many a-words keep /æ/ even in SSBE: cat, bad, trap, man, map, back, flat,
hand, land, stand, random, gas, pan, bank, cash. The split is specific to the
environments above, and even then has exceptions (e.g. "mass", "gas", "lass"
stay /æ/; "ant", "romance" vary).

## Practice tip

When you meet a new a-word in these environments, default to /ɑː/ for SSBE and
/æ/ for American. For "-ance/-ant" the split is less consistent, so check.

## Common Spanish error

> The trap: using one short, front, Spanish "a" everywhere, missing the long
> back /ɑː/ entirely.
> The fix: learn the families above as sets. The S/TH/F groups are the most
> consistent; master those first.
