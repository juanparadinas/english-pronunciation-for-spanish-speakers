# R and W break the vowel rules

> Two letters systematically distort the normal spelling-to-sound rules: R and
> W. They change the vowel that comes with them, so the usual syllable rules
> (closed = short, etc.) do not apply. This confuses Spanish speakers badly,
> because the spelling looks regular but the sound is not.

## Why it matters

The [syllable rules](spelling-to-sound-rules.md) predict vowels well, UNTIL an
R or a W is involved. Then:
- R colours and usually lengthens the preceding vowel; vowel+r rarely sounds
  like the plain vowel.
- W rounds or backs a following a, and flips some r-combinations.

Learn these as overrides to the normal rules.

## R effects

### u + r -> /ɜː/ (not the closed /ʌ/)
A u in a closed syllable is normally /ʌ/ (cut, bus, sun). But u + r flips it to
the long schwa /ɜː/, not /ʌ/ + r:

cut /ʌ/ -> curt /ɜː/; bun -> burn; cub -> curb; hut -> hurt; tun -> turn.
Also: nurse, purse, curse, church, further, turtle, disturb, Thursday.

So the closed-syllable rule is overridden by the r. This is a top Spanish trap:
they say turn as "turn" with /ʌ/ + a rolled r.

### vowel + r in general (r-controlled syllables)
Every vowel changes before r:
- a + r -> /ɑː/ (car, start), but a + r + e -> /eə/ (care), and intervocalic
  a + r -> /eə/ (vary); see [more spelling rules](more-spelling-rules.md).
- e/i/u + r (stressed) -> /ɜː/ (her, bird, turn).
- o + r -> /ɔː/ (short, born).
See each vowel page and [long schwa /ɜː/](vowel-long-schwa.md).

### double rr closes the syllable
arr -> short /æ/ (carry) vs single ar + vowel -> /eə/ (vary); urr -> UK /ʌr/
(hurry) vs US /ɜːr/. Full detail in [double consonants](double-consonants.md).

## W effects

### w + a -> /ɒ/ (not /æ/)
After w (and qu), a written a is rounded to /ɒ/, not the expected front /æ/:

want, wash, watch, swan, swamp, wander, what; quality, quantity, squad,
quarrel, quarantine. Compare "cat" /æ/ with "watch" /ɒ/.

### w + or -> /ɜː/ (not /ɔː/)
"or" is normally /ɔː/ (for, short, born). After w it becomes /ɜː/:

work, word, world, worm, worse, worth, worship. Exceptions keep /ɔː/: worn,
sworn.

### w + ar -> /ɔː/ (not /ɑː/)
"ar" is normally /ɑː/ (car, far). After w it becomes /ɔː/:

war, warm, ward, warn, dwarf, reward, quarter, towards.

So w pulls the vowel one way (a -> /ɒ/, ar -> /ɔː/, or -> /ɜː/); none follow
the plain rule.

## Summary table

| Sequence | Normal rule | With R/W override | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| u (closed) | /ʌ/ | u + r -> /ɜː/ | cut vs curt, turn, burn |
| a | /æ/ | w/qu + a -> /ɒ/ | cat vs watch, want, squad |
| ar | /ɑː/ | w + ar -> /ɔː/ | car vs war, warm, ward |
| or | /ɔː/ | w + or -> /ɜː/ | for vs work, word, world |

## Common Spanish error

> The trap: applying the normal syllable rule and reading turn as /tʌrn/,
> watch as /wætʃ/, or work as /wɔːrk/, usually with a rolled Spanish r.
> The fix: treat R and W as rule-breakers. u+r = /ɜː/; w+a = /ɒ/; w+ar = /ɔː/;
> w+or = /ɜː/. And the English r has no roll (see [/r/](cons-r.md)).
