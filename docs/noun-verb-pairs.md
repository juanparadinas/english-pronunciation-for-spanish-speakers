# Noun/verb pairs (same spelling, different pronunciation)

> Many English words are spelled identically as a noun/adjective and as a verb
> but pronounced differently. The difference is one of two mechanisms: a STRESS
> SHIFT (which also reduces the unstressed vowel), or a SOUND CHANGE (a
> consonant or vowel changes) with no stress shift. Classify each pair; do not
> assume they all shift.

Summary table also lives on [word stress](stress.md); this page is the full
treatment.

## The two mechanisms

1. Stress shift: the stress moves between syllables, and the now-unstressed
   syllable reduces to schwa. Example: REcord /ˈrekɔːd/ (noun) vs reCORD
   /rɪˈkɔːd/ (verb), the first vowel goes from /e/ to /ɪ/ (reduced).
2. Sound change without stress shift: a consonant voices or a vowel changes,
   but the stress stays. Example: use /juːs/ (noun) vs use /juːz/ (verb).

## Class A — Standard shifters (noun/adjective 1st, verb 2nd)

The classic set. Noun/adjective stresses syllable 1; verb stresses syllable 2.
The unstressed syllable reduces, so the vowels differ too.

## Class B — Always first syllable (no shift)

Both noun and verb stress syllable 1; no change. Many have the stress-neutral
-ment ending or are simply fixed.

comment, promise, focus, profit, picture, visit, answer, programme, process
(UK), travel, label, offer, order, murder, promise, purchase, witness,
comfort, credit, limit, benefit, balance, damage.

Note on endings: the -ment noun suffix is stress-neutral (COMment,
GOVernment, ARGument), which is why "comment" stays on the first syllable. But
-ent in general is NOT always unstressed, many words stress it (eVENT,
preVENT, reSENT, inVENT, perCENT), so do not over-generalise from -ment.

## Class C — Always second syllable (no shift)

Both noun and verb stress syllable 2; no change.

report, request, result, release, respect, review, reward, surprise, concern,
mistake, control, command, demand, support, account, debate, delay, design,
dislike, escape, exchange, reply, research (UK also REsearch).

## Class D — Sound change (not stress) and irregular pairs

Here the pronunciation changes but the stress usually does NOT move. Often a
voicing change (see the [/s/-/z/ alternation](cons-z.md)) or a vowel change.

### Voicing: noun/adjective /s/ vs verb /z/
The final /s/ voices to /z/ in the verb; stress unchanged.

| Word | Noun/adj /s/ | Verb /z/ |
|---|---|---|
| use | /juːs/ | /juːz/ |
| house | /haʊs/ | /haʊz/ |
| close | /kləʊs/ (adj) | /kləʊz/ |
| abuse | /əˈbjuːs/ | /əˈbjuːz/ |
| excuse | /ɪkˈskjuːs/ | /ɪkˈskjuːz/ |
| diffuse | /dɪˈfjuːs/ (adj) | /dɪˈfjuːz/ |
| misuse | /ˌmɪsˈjuːs/ | /ˌmɪsˈjuːz/ |
| grease | /griːs/ | /griːz/ (variable) |

Spelled to mark it: advice/advise, device/devise, licence/license (UK),
practice/practise (UK). See the [/s/-/z/ alternation](cons-z.md).

### Voicing: noun /θ/ vs verb /ð/
The spelling often marks it: -th noun, -the verb. Several also change the vowel.

| Noun /θ/ | Verb /ð/ |
|---|---|
| bath /bɑːθ/ | bathe /beɪð/ (vowel change too) |
| breath /breθ/ | breathe /briːð/ (vowel change too) |
| cloth /klɒθ/ | clothe /kləʊð/ (vowel change too) |
| teeth /tiːθ/ | teethe /tiːð/ |
| sheath /ʃiːθ/ | sheathe /ʃiːð/ |
| wreath /riːθ/ | wreathe /riːð/ |
| mouth /maʊθ/ | mouth (verb) /maʊð/ |
| loath /ləʊθ/ (adj) | loathe /ləʊð/ |

See [/θ/](cons-th-voiceless.md), [/ð/](cons-th-voiced.md).

### Voicing: noun /f/ vs verb /v/

| deliberate | /dɪˈlɪbərət/ | /dɪˈlɪbəreɪt/ |

### Heteronyms (same spelling, different word/sense, different sound)
These are NOT strictly noun-vs-verb; they are same-spelled words with
unrelated senses and different pronunciations. Listed because they belong to
the same "spelling lies" phenomenon.

| Word | Reading 1 | Reading 2 |
|---|---|---|
| lead | /liːd/ (to guide, verb) | /led/ (the metal, noun) |
| read | /riːd/ (present) | /red/ (past) |
| wind | /wɪnd/ (air, noun) | /waɪnd/ (to turn, verb) |
| tear | /tɪə/ (crying, noun) | /teə/ (to rip, verb) |
| bow | /bəʊ/ (ribbon/weapon) | /baʊ/ (to bend) |
| minute | /ˈmɪnɪt/ (time, noun) | /maɪˈnjuːt/ (tiny, adj) |
| row | /rəʊ/ (line / to row) | /raʊ/ (a quarrel) |
| close | /kləʊs/ (near, adj) | /kləʊz/ (to shut, verb) |
| content | /ˈkɒntent/ (what's inside, noun) | /kənˈtent/ (satisfied, adj) |

Some heteronyms (content, close) overlap with the earlier classes; they are
repeated here to show the full "one spelling, two sounds" set.

## How to decide

1. Is it in the standard shifter set (Class A)? Then noun -> 1st, verb -> 2nd,
   and reduce the unstressed syllable.
2. If not, check whether it is a fixed first (Class B) or second (Class C)
   word, no change.
3. Watch for the sound-change pairs (Class D): use/use, house/house,
   separate, bath/bathe, where the stress stays but a sound changes.

## Common Spanish error

> The trap: using one pronunciation for both the noun and the verb, or
> assuming every same-spelled pair shifts stress.
> The fix: learn the standard shifter set (record, present, contract), and
> treat the sound-change pairs (use, house, close, separate) separately, their
> stress does not move.
