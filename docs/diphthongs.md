# Diphthongs (8 sounds)

> A diphthong is one vowel that glides into another inside a single syllable.
> English has 8. Spanish has diphthongs too, but the English ones differ in
> quality and in the glide, and the British centring diphthongs (ending in
> schwa) do not exist in Spanish.

## 1. What they are

Eight gliding vowels. Three groups:

- Closing to /ɪ/: /eɪ/, /aɪ/, /ɔɪ/
- Closing to /ʊ/: /aʊ/, /əʊ/
- Centring to /ə/ (British, from a dropped R): /ɪə/, /eə/, /ʊə/

Spanish contrast: the centring diphthongs (/ɪə/, /eə/, /ʊə/) are the hard ones.
They exist in SSBE because the final R is not pronounced and becomes a schwa.
Spanish speakers either pronounce the R or use a pure vowel.

## 2. How to produce them

Start on the first vowel and glide smoothly toward the second; do not break it
into two syllables. For centring diphthongs, glide into a relaxed schwa and do
not pronounce the R (British).

## 6. Common Spanish error

> The trap 1: pronouncing the written R in centring diphthongs (here, care,
> tour), which breaks the British sound.
> The trap 2: /əʊ/ said as a pure Spanish "o" (go = "gou" with Spanish o).
> The fix: glide into a schwa, no R (British). For /əʊ/, start from schwa, not
> from "o" (this is the main UK/US difference too: US uses /oʊ/).
