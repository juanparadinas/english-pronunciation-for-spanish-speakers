# Connected speech and phonology

What happens to a vowel inside a word (unstressed → schwa) also happens at the
sentence level: whole function words weaken, and content words carry the
stress. English is stress-timed; Spanish is syllable-timed. This changes
everything about rhythm and intelligibility.

## Linking

Semivowels inserted between words to link them: yod /j/, R, and W. Plus the
general linking of final consonant to following vowel.

## Assimilation

Adjacent sounds influence each other (e.g. /dj/ → /dʒ/ in "during").

## Elision

Sounds dropped in connected speech.

## Geminates / twinning

Two identical adjacent sounds merge.

## T and D phenomena

- **Drop T**: common in connected speech.
- **Glottal T stop**: replaces T in many positions; heavy in some British
  accents. Obama's "certain" is a cited example.
- **Drop D**: e.g. "and" → /ən/; friend/friends in fast speech.
- **Stop plosives** / unreleased plosives.
- **American T**: flap t in unstressed syllables.

## Drop H

Function words with H lose it in connected speech (him, her, have).

## Weak forms

Function words have a weak (schwa) form in connected speech:
- was, been, from, of/off, and, can/can't.
Content words keep strong forms; function words reduce. Reductions and
contractions are very important.

## Rhythm and intonation

- Stress-timed rhythm: stressed syllables fall at regular intervals;
  unstressed syllables compress.
- Intonation changes meaning; thought groups chunk the utterance.
- Uptalk is a notable intonation pattern.

## Consonant clusters

More common in English than Spanish. Two typical Spanish errors:
1. Inserting an E before initial S-clusters (school, Spain).
2. Mispronouncing final consonants and final clusters (rare in Spanish).

## Dark L

table, travel, trouble, pencil, level, equal, model, nasal, annual, moral,
royal, label, cancel, unveil, capitol, methanol, alcohol, control, lawful.
Distorts adjacent vowels heavily.
