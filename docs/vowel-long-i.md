# Vowel: long i /iː/

> The tense, long counterpart of /ɪ/. Spanish speakers produce it fairly well,
> but must keep it distinct from /ɪ/.

## 1. What it is

/iː/ is a long, tense, high front vowel, close to the Spanish "i" but longer
and tenser. It is the vowel in "sheep", "beat", "see".

Spanish contrast: close to Spanish /i/, so it is one of the easier sounds. The
real work is keeping it separate from the short lax /ɪ/.

## 2. How to produce it

Tongue high and front, lips spread as if smiling, held long and tense. More
tension and length than Spanish "i".

## 3. Angle 1 — when a spelling makes /iː/

| Spelling | Examples |
|---|---|
| ee | see, sheep, feet, green, tree |
| ea | beat, sea, please, reason, leave, easy |
| e (open syllable) | he, me, she, these, complete |
| ie | field, believe, piece |
| ei (after c) | receive, ceiling, deceive |
| i (some loanwords) | machine, police, ski, pizza |
| y (final) | happy, city, funny (weak, between /i/ and /ɪ/) |

## 4. Angle 2 — where the /iː/ sound is written

ee: see, sheep, feet, green, tree, agree.
ea: beat, sea, please, reason, leave, easy, meat, team.
e / e-e: he, me, she, these, complete, eve.
ie: field, believe, piece, chief.
ei: receive, ceiling, deceive, seize.
i: machine, police, ski, litre, pizza, visa.

## 5. Minimal pairs / contrasts

See the full /ɪ/ vs /iː/ table on [short i /ɪ/](vowel-short-i.md):
ship/sheep, bit/beat, live/leave, sit/seat, fit/feet, rich/reach, it/eat.

## 6. Common Spanish error

> The trap: collapsing /iː/ and /ɪ/ into one Spanish "i", so sheep = ship.
> The fix: /iː/ is long and tense (smile); /ɪ/ is short and lax (relaxed).
> Always train as a pair.
