# Consonant: /v/

> Does not exist in Spanish. Spanish b and v are the same sound, so speakers
> collapse /b/ and /v/.

## 1. What it is

/v/ is a voiced labiodental fricative: top teeth on the bottom lip, with voice
and friction. The sound in "very", "love", "van".

Spanish contrast: in Spanish, b and v are pronounced identically (both a
bilabial /b/ or /β/). Spanish speakers say /b/ where English needs /v/, so
"very" becomes "berry".

## 2. How to produce it

Rest the upper teeth lightly on the lower lip, push voiced air through so it
buzzes. The lips do NOT come together (that would make /b/). It is the voiced
partner of /f/.

## 3. Angle 1 — when a spelling makes /v/

| Spelling | Examples |
|---|---|
| v | very, love, van, have, give, voice, service |
| f (in "of") | of (/ɒv/) |
| ph (rare) | Stephen |

## 4. Angle 2 — where the /v/ sound is written

Almost always v: very, love, van, have, give, voice, service, over, never,
value, available, develop, visible, vowel, invest.
Exception: "of" is /ɒv/. "Stephen" has /v/ for ph.

## 5. Minimal pairs / contrasts

The /b/ vs /v/ contrast is the core problem.

| /v/ | /b/ |
|---|---|
| very | berry |
| van | ban |
| vest | best |
| vote | boat |
| vowel | bowel |
| curve | curb |

Also /v/ vs /f/ (voicing): van/fan, vine/fine, save/safe.

## 6. Common Spanish error

> The trap: pronouncing v as a Spanish b, so very = berry, vote = boat.
> The fix: teeth on lip, buzz. Keep the lips apart. Train /v/-/b/ pairs
> (very/berry, van/ban) until they are reliably different.
