# Vowel: short o /ɒ/

> A short, rounded, open-back vowel. This is the clearest UK/US vowel
> difference: American replaces it with a back /ɑ/.

## 1. What it is

/ɒ/ is a short, rounded, open back vowel, the sound in "hot", "lost", "dock"
in SSBE. It exists in British English but not in General American.

Spanish contrast: Spanish "o" is more closed and not this open. Spanish
speakers use a tenser "o". American speakers use an unrounded back /ɑ/.

## 2. How to produce it

Open the mouth, tongue low and back, with light lip rounding. Short. Less
rounded and more open than Spanish "o".

## 3. Angle 1 — when a spelling makes /ɒ/

| Spelling | Examples |
|---|---|
| o (closed syllable) | hot, lost, dock, stock, top, box, god, clock |
| a (after w/qu) | want, wash, watch, quality, squad |
| ou (rare) | cough, trough |

## 4. Angle 2 — where the /ɒ/ sound is written

o (closed): hot, lost, dock, stock, top, box, god, clock, shop, rock, odd.
a (after w, qu): want, wash, watch, what, quality, squad, quantity.
ou: cough, trough.
au (some, UK): because (variant), sausage.

## 5. Minimal pairs / contrasts

| Pair | Contrast |
|---|---|
| hot / hat | /ɒ/ vs /æ/ |
| lock / luck | /ɒ/ vs /ʌ/ |
| cot / caught | /ɒ/ vs /ɔː/ |
| pot / port | /ɒ/ vs /ɔː/ |
| not / nought | /ɒ/ vs /ɔː/ |

## 6. Common Spanish error

> The trap: a closed, tense Spanish "o".
> The fix: open the mouth more and round the lips only lightly. Keep it short.
> Note: in American this sound becomes an unrounded back /ɑ/ (hot = "haht").
