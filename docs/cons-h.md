# Consonant: /h/

> A simple breath sound, but Spanish speakers either drop it or replace it with
> the harsh Spanish "j".

## 1. What it is

/h/ is a voiceless glottal fricative, just a push of breath, as in "house",
"behind", "ahead".

Spanish contrast: the Spanish letter h is silent, and the Spanish "j" (jamón)
is a harsh velar sound. Spanish speakers either drop the English /h/ entirely
or replace it with that harsh /x/.

## 2. How to produce it

Simply breathe out audibly before the vowel, with the mouth already in position
for that vowel. No friction at the back of the throat, no Spanish "j". It is
light.

## 3. Angle 1 — when a spelling makes /h/

| Spelling | Pronounced? | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| h before a vowel | yes | house, happy, behind, ahead, hello |
| h in wh- (some) | yes (/h/) | who, whole, whose |
| silent h | no | hour, honest, heir, vehicle |
| function words (weak) | often dropped | him, her, have (in connected speech) |

## 4. Angle 2 — where the /h/ sound is written

h: house, happy, behind, ahead, hello, help, hand, home, perhaps, ahead.
wh (some): who, whole, whose, whom.

### h is pronounced by default; silent only in a short list
The useful framing: a written h before a vowel is pronounced, EXCEPT in these
common silent-h words (learn them as a set):

- hour, honest, honour, heir (and derivatives: honestly, heirloom).
- vehicle, vehement, annihilate.
- ghost, ghastly (gh), rhythm, rhyme (rh).
- -ham place names: Durham, Nottingham, Birmingham, Chatham.
- French loans: herb (US silent; UK pronounced), homage (varies).

Everywhere else, pronounce the h: history, hospital, hotel, horrible, human.
See [silent letters](silent-letters.md) for the full silent-letter patterns.

## 6. Common Spanish error

> The trap 1: dropping /h/ so "house" = "ouse".
> The trap 2: using the harsh Spanish "j" (/x/) so "hello" sounds throaty.
> The fix: a light breath only. Contrast hair/air and heat/eat; the only
> difference is a soft puff of air.
