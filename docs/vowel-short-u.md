# Vowel: short u /ʊ/

> A short, lax, rounded, high-back vowel. The closed-syllable exceptions
> (put, push, bull) and the /uː/ contrast are the traps.

## 1. What it is

/ʊ/ is a short, lax, rounded high-back vowel, the sound in "put", "book",
"could". The short, relaxed counterpart of /uː/.

Spanish contrast: Spanish "u" is tense. Spanish speakers use that tense /u/ and
fail to distinguish /ʊ/ from /uː/ (full/fool).

## 2. How to produce it

Tongue high and back, light lip rounding, relaxed and short. Looser and shorter
than /uː/.

## 3. Angle 1 — when a spelling makes /ʊ/

| Spelling | Examples |
|---|---|
| u (closed exceptions) | put, push, bull, pull, full, bullet |
| oo | book, good, foot, look, wood, cook |
| ou | could, should, would |
| o (rare) | woman, wolf |

## 4. Angle 2 — where the /ʊ/ sound is written

u (exceptions to the /ʌ/ rule): put, push, bull, pull, full, bullet.
oo: book, good, foot, look, wood, cook, stood, hook.
ou: could, should, would.
o: woman, wolf.
Weak -ure endings (variant): exposure, measure, culture (schwa or /ʊ/).

## 5. Minimal pairs / contrasts

| Pair | Contrast |
|---|---|
| full / fool | /ʊ/ vs /uː/ |
| pull / pool | /ʊ/ vs /uː/ |
| look / Luke | /ʊ/ vs /uː/ |
| could / cooed | /ʊ/ vs /uː/ |
| put / putt | /ʊ/ vs /ʌ/ |

## 6. Common Spanish error

> The trap: using tense Spanish /u/ for both /ʊ/ and /uː/, so full = fool.
> The fix: /ʊ/ is short and lax; /uː/ is long and tense. Remember the
> closed-syllable exceptions (put, push, bull, pull) that take /ʊ/, not /ʌ/.
