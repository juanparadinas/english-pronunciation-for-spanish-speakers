# Phoneme inventory

> English has far more sounds than Spanish: around 44 phonemes (24 consonants,
> 20 vowels/diphthongs) versus roughly 24 in Spanish. This page is the map;
> each sound links to its own page.

One traditional count: 24 consonants, 12 vowels, 8 diphthongs,
5 triphthongs = 49 (counting triphthongs and some glides). The standard SSBE
count is ~44 phonemes. Either way, the point stands: many more than Spanish.

## Consonants shared with Spanish (lower priority)

/p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /k/, /g/, /f/, /s/, /m/, /n/, /l/ (light), /j/, /dʒ/.
These broadly map to Spanish sounds and need less work, though placement and
aspiration (p/t/k) differ.

## Why the inventory matters

Learning the sounds that do not exist in Spanish is the highest-leverage work.
Start with the priority vowels (schwa, long schwa, short i, long back /ɑː/) and the priority
consonants (r, dark l, v, z, sh, h). See
[priority phonemes](priority-phonemes.md).
