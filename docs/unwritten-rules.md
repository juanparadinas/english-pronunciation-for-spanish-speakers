# Unwritten rules

The reliable patterns that native children absorb but that are never written in
a grammar for learners. They fail sometimes but hold in a great many cases.
For the written syllable rules (closed, open, magic-e, R-controlled, vowel
team, C+le) see [spelling-to-sound rules](spelling-to-sound-rules.md).

## Rule 1 — yod drop after L or R

Open-syllable U is /juː/, but the yod drops after L or R, even in British:
conclusion, include (L), rude, blue (R). Americans drop it far more widely
(tune, duke, student, news). Full treatment in [the letter U](letter-u.md).

## Rule 2 — initial unstressed E = short /ɪ/

Word starts consonant + E, and the 2nd syllable is stressed: that E becomes a
short /ɪ/, almost always.

regarding, reverse, description, result, deserve, redundancy, enable, defect,
effect, default, evolve, severe, device, security, delivery, evaluation, event,
credential, present, report, decision, encrypt.

## Rule 3 — -AW / -AU = /ɔː/

Always an O sound, never the Spanish "au". American often turns it into a back
/ɑ/.

default, caught, taught, faulty, launch, cause, because, clause, pause,
australia, author, audience, august, autumn, audit, authorization, audio.

## Rule 4 — suffix -ate: noun/adjective vs verb

| Part of speech | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| noun / adjective | /ət/ | candidate, certificate, climate, appropriate, delegate (n) |
| verb | /eɪt/ | integrate, fluctuate, separate (v), delegate (v) |

## Rule 5 — noun/adjective vs verb stress shift

Same spelling; the standard shifters stress syllable 1 as a noun/adjective and
syllable 2 as a verb, with the vowels reducing accordingly:

PROgress / proGRESS, REcord / reCORD, PREsent / preSENT, CONtract / conTRACT,
PROject / proJECT, EXport / exPORT, REbel / reBEL, PERmit / perMIT.

Caution: not every same-spelled pair shifts. "comment", "request", "command"
do not. And some pairs change a SOUND rather than the stress (use /juːs/ vs
/juːz/, house, close). Full four-class treatment in
[noun/verb pairs](noun-verb-pairs.md).

## Rule 6 — which spellings hide /ʃ/, /ʒ/, /z/

- /ʃ/: not only sh, also -tion, -ssion, -cious, -cial, -cian, s+u (sure).
- /ʒ/: -sion after a vowel (vision), -sure (measure), genre.
- /z/: s between vowels (easy), is/was/as/his, -ize/-ise, ex- (exam, example).

See [/ʃ/](cons-sh.md), [/ʒ/](cons-zh.md), [/z/](cons-z.md).

## Rule 7 — free variation of the schwa

In some unstressed syllables the schwa can be replaced by short /ɪ/ or /ʊ/
(mostly /ɪ/). The distance is tiny; it does not matter. Never use a full
Spanish vowel. See [schwa](vowel-schwa.md).

## Rule 8 — unstable vowel teams OU / UI / EA

No fixed rule; each word is learned. OU has ~11 values (see
[the letters OU](letter-ou.md)); EA varies (beat /iː/, head /e/, great /eɪ/,
heart /ɑː/).
