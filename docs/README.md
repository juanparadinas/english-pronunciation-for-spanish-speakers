# English Pronunciation (SSBE)

Personal project: a structured method to teach and improve English
pronunciation, aimed at native Spanish speakers. Reference accent is SSBE
(Southern Standard British English, also called RP / BBC / Queen's English),
with the main differences to General American noted throughout.

## Origin story

I started years ago to improve my English, especially my speaking. I realised
the first thing I had to do was learn pronunciation.

Everyone talks about comprehensible input, how children learn, and it makes
sense. But my own experience, and what I saw in others, is that no matter how
many series and films you watch, the improvement is slight, especially in
speaking and listening. There are things we do not understand when natives
speak. It is not magic. So I decided to learn pronunciation properly.

Where do you start? With the vowels; they are the most important phonemes in
any language. I had heard English has twelve to fourteen vowels; it sounded
crazy. I watched general articulation videos on YouTube, then went vowel by
vowel through all twelve. A big jump. Then the consonants: English has around
ten or eleven that do not exist in Spanish. That opened up a world that blew
my mind.

With the phonemes in place I focused on words, and understood how important the
schwa is: the weakening of unstressed syllables, a phenomenon that does not
exist in Spanish. Then, just when I thought I had words under control, I
discovered connected speech, linking, assimilation, elision, which changes
everything. The same weakening inside a word happens across whole phrases:
function words reduce, content words carry the stress.

IPA decision: I considered learning the IPA formally but chose not to; it
seemed too complex. Yet as I studied each phoneme I learned the IPA implicitly.
It is now my best tool: I look up a word's transcription in a dictionary and I
know how it is pronounced without hearing it.

English is not phonetic; it is not written as it is spoken. So the real
question is: given a written word, how do you know how to say it? There are
rules, around eight written ones and many unwritten ones. They fail sometimes
but hold in a great many cases. This project collects them in one place.

## Why this exists

Comprehensible input alone (watching series, films) improved speaking and
listening only slightly. The jump came from studying pronunciation explicitly,
bottom-up: phonemes first, then words, then connected speech, then rhythm and
intonation.

Inventory learned: 24 consonants, 12 vowels, 8 diphthongs, 5 triphthongs. 49
phonemes total, many more than Spanish.

IPA decision: did not study IPA formally. Learning each phoneme implicitly
taught it. IPA is now the primary tool: look up the dictionary transcription
and the pronunciation is known without hearing the word.

## The learning path

1. Vowels. The most important phonemes in any language.
2. Consonants. Focus on the 10-11 that do not exist in Spanish.
3. Syllables and words. Stress and the schwa in unstressed syllables.
4. Connected speech. Linking, assimilation, elision.
5. Sentence level. Weak forms, content vs function words.
6. Rhythm and intonation. English is stress-timed; Spanish is syllable-timed.

## Core principle

The biggest error for Spanish speakers is forcing a full vowel into every
syllable, as written, instead of using the schwa. Even schwa deletion is
something Spanish speakers never do. Prioritize the schwa above everything.
