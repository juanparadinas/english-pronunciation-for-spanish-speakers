# Spelling-to-sound rules

English is not phonetic: you cannot always predict pronunciation from spelling.
But reliable rules exist. Native children learn them; non-natives are not
taught them. They fail sometimes, yet hold in a large share of cases. Always
read the mapping both ways: given a letter, which sound; given a sound, which
spelling.

This page covers the **written syllable rules**. The **unwritten rules** (yod
drop, initial E, suffixes, stress shift, hidden sibilants) are in
[unwritten-rules.md](unwritten-rules.md). Per-letter two-angle pages:
[A](letter-a.md), [U](letter-u.md), [OU](letter-ou.md).

## The six written syllable rules

| Rule | What happens | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Closed syllable | Vowel is short. | bed, dog, went, top, exist, request |
| Open syllable | Vowel says its alphabet name. | go, he, me, she, admire, stupid |
| Magic e (CVCe) | e is silent; vowel says its name. | make, rate, like, bake, cube |
| R-controlled | Vowel coloured by R. | car, short, her, first, turn, learn |
| Vowel team | Two vowels; often first is long. | brain, meat, please, goal, receive |
| Consonant + le (C+le) | Final syllabic l. | table, castle, purple |

### Closed syllable
A single vowel followed by one or more consonants in the same syllable; the
vowel is short: bed, dog, went, top, because, conference, exist, request, event.

### Open syllable
A syllable ending in the vowel; it says its alphabet name: go, he, me, she,
information, admire, stupid, exposure.

### Magic e
Consonant + vowel + consonant + e; the e is silent and the vowel says its
name: make, rate, like, bake. Full word lists by vowel in
[reference word lists](reference-word-lists.md).

### R-controlled
The R changes the vowel:
- ar: car, start, far
- or: short, port, cord
- ir / ur / ear / er / our: her, first, turn, curve, learn, permanent, journey,
  nurse, further

### Vowel team
Two vowels acting together: container, brain, straight, pain, nail, day, lead,
meat, sea, reason, please, leave, easy, see, receive, goal, coast, approach,
goes, tuesday, venue, due, argue.

Important: a vowel team is almost never read as two Spanish vowels. It spells
one sound (a long vowel or a diphthong). See
[more spelling rules](more-spelling-rules.md) for which teams resemble their
spelling (ay/ai/ey, oy/oi, ou/ow), the British /əʊ/ = schwa+ʊ trap, and the
highly irregular "ei" team.

### Consonant + le
A final syllabic l: table, castle, purple, little, apple.

## How to use the rules

1. Split the word into syllables.
2. Classify each syllable (closed, open, magic-e, R-controlled, vowel team,
   C+le).
3. Apply the rule for the vowel.
4. Watch for R and W, which override the normal rules (see
   [R and W break the vowel rules](r-and-w-distortions.md)).
5. Check against the unwritten rules for suffixes, stress, and the hidden
   sibilants.
