    Word stress¶

    Stress is the backbone of English pronunciation. Get the stress right and a word is usually intelligible even with imperfect sounds; get it wrong and correct sounds still fail. Find the stress first, then everything else follows.

    How to pronounce an unknown word (the procedure)¶

    1. Find the stressed syllable (rules below).
    2. Pronounce the stressed syllable with full value, applying the spelling-to- sound rules (syllable rules, letter pages).
    3. Reduce every unstressed syllable to a schwa (or a short /ɪ/ / /ʊ/ by free variation). Never give an unstressed syllable its full Spanish vowel. The vowel letter hints at which reduced sound: a/o/u lean schwa, i leans /ɪ/, e leans /ɪ/ (see schwa: which reduced sound).
    4. Apply connected-speech changes if the word is in a phrase (connected speech).

    This is the single most useful habit: stress first, vowels second, reduction everywhere else.

    Default: two-syllable words¶

    Most English words are two syllables, and the default depends on word class:

    Word class Stress Examples
    Nouns 1st syllable TAble, WAter, DOCtor, MOUNtain, CARpet
    Adjectives 1st syllable HAPpy, YELlow, CLEVer, BASic
    Verbs 2nd syllable deCIDE, reLAX, forGET, aRRIVE, begIN

    So the same two syllables lean one way for a noun/adjective and the other for a verb. This is a tendency, not a law; there are many exceptions (e.g. the verb "VIsit", "PROmise" stress the first; the noun "hoTEL", "maCHINE" stress the second).

    Noun/adjective vs verb: same spelling, different stress¶

    A set of words shift stress by class: the noun/adjective stresses syllable 1, the verb stresses syllable 2. Vowels reduce accordingly. This is a summary; the full four-class treatment (standard shifters, always-first, always-second, and sound-change pairs like use/use and house/house) is in noun/verb pairs.

    Word Noun/adj Verb
    record REcord reCORD
    progress PROgress proGRESS
    present PREsent preSENT
    contract CONtract conTRACT
    project PROject proJECT
    rebel REbel reBEL
    export EXport exPORT
    permit PERmit perMIT
    conduct CONduct conDUCT
    object OBject obJECT
    increase INcrease inCREASE

    Caveats: - Not all same-spelled pairs shift: "comment", "promise", "focus", "profit" keep first-syllable stress for both noun and verb. - Some can be stressed either way without changing meaning much (regional or free variation): "address", "research", "detail" (noun) vary by accent (UK often REsearch vs US reSEARCH). - Learn the common shifting set; do not assume every pair shifts.

    Suffix-driven stress¶

    Many suffixes fix the stress position. The most reliable:

    Suffix Stress falls on Examples
    -tion, -sion syllable before the suffix eduCAtion, deCIsion, naTIONal (penult)
    -ic syllable before -ic eCOnomic, fanTAStic, phoTOgraphic
    -ity syllable before -ity aBIlity, possiBIlity, actiVIty
    -ial, -ian syllable before it esSENtial, muSIcian
    -ious, -eous syllable before it inDUStrious, outRAgeous
    -ify third from end iDENtify, CLAssify

    Rule of thumb for -tion/-sion/-ic/-ity/-ial: stress the syllable immediately before the suffix (the penultimate syllable of the whole word).

    Suffixes usually change vowel quality, NOT stress¶

    Most derivational suffixes are unstressed: they often change the suffix vowel (schwa <-> full) while the stress stays on the root. The clearest case is -ate verbs:

    • -ate: noun/adjective /ət/ vs verb /eɪt/, but the stress does not move. DElegate (n /ət/) -> DElegate (v /eɪt/); ESTimate, SEParate, MODerate, ADvocate, DUPlicate, APProximate, GRADuate, ALTernate, arTICulate, deLIBerate, eLABorate, asSOCiate. Only the suffix vowel changes; the stressed syllable is unchanged.
    • -ine (unstressed /ɪn/, not /aɪn/): deTERmine, eXAMine, iMAGine, GENuine, MEDicine, DIScipline, FAMine, enGINE. Stress on the root, suffix reduced.
    • Other usually-unstressed endings: -age (VILLage, MANage, PACKage), -ace (PALace, SURface, MENace), -ous (FAMous, DANGerous), -ure (NATure, CULture), -ive (NEGative, OBjective keeps root stress).

    Takeaway: when a word has one of these endings, find the stress on the ROOT, then reduce the suffix. The suffix is a passenger, not the driver.

    Self-stressed suffixes (the exceptions: these DO take the stress themselves): -ee (emploYEE, trainEE, refuGEE, guaranTEE), -eer (engiNEER, volunTEER, pioNEER), -ese (japaNESE, portuGUESE, chiNESE), -ette (cigaRETTE, kitcheNETTE), -esque (pictuRESQUE, groTESQUE), -oon (balLOON, carTOON), -aire (millionAIRE), -ade (lemonADE).

    Stress-neutral suffixes (do not move the stress of the base): -ly, -ness, -ment, -ful, -less, -er, -ing, -ed. So HAPpy -> HAPpily, GOVern -> GOVernment.

    R-controlled syllables attract stress¶

    An r-coloured syllable (vowel + r giving /ɜː/, /ɑː/, /ɔː/) is heavy and tends to pull the stress onto itself. Useful as a tie-breaker when you are unsure which syllable is stressed: the r-syllable is a strong candidate. Works across word classes and word lengths, not just two-syllable verbs.

    • Verbs (2-syl): preFER, reCUR, conCERN, deTER, aLERT, deSERVE, obSERVE, reTURN, disTURB, preSERVE, conVERT, eMERGE, inCUR, aVERT.
    • Verbs (3+ syl): deTERmine, disCERN, reaFFIRM, misinTERpret (on -ter-), reHEARSE, reiMBURSE.
    • Nouns (2-syl): conCERN, reTURN, deSSERT (/z/), reWARD, suPPORT.
    • Nouns (3+ syl): perFORMance, imPORtance, dePARture, disORder, obSERver, conVERsion, deTERminant.
    • Adjectives: abSURD, aLERT, perVERSE, suPERB, eTERnal, inFORmal, exTERnal (on -ter-), noCTURNal (on -tur-), deTERminate.
    • Longer words where the r-syllable carries primary stress: perFORMance, conSERvative (on -ser-), obSERvatory (on -ser-), alTERNative (on -ter-), comFORTable (on -for-), deTERmination (secondary on -ter-, primary -na-).

    This is a tendency, not a law. Counter-cases where the stress is elsewhere: WAter, MANner, CORner, MURmur, GOVernor (first syllable). And suffix-driven words still obey their suffix rule (uniVERsity stresses -ver- by the -ity rule, which here happens to be the r-syllable too).

    Stress shift under suffixation¶

    Adding a suffix can move the stress and change every vowel:

    PHOtograph -> phoTOgraphy -> photoGRAPHic. POLitics -> poLItical -> politiCIAN PERson -> perSONify

    This is why the same root sounds so different across its family: the stress moves, and the vowels reduce around the new stressed syllable.

    Primary and secondary stress¶

    Long words (often those with suffixes) have two stresses: - Primary stress: the strongest syllable (marked ˈ in IPA). - Secondary stress: a weaker beat earlier in the word (marked ˌ), often where the base word used to be stressed.

    Examples: ˌpronunciˈation, ˌuniverˈsity (no), ˌeduˈcation, ˌpossiˈbility, ˌinterˈnational. The secondary stress keeps long words rhythmic; the syllables between the two stresses reduce to schwa.

    Common Spanish error¶

    The trap: flat, even stress (Spanish is syllable-timed), or stressing the wrong syllable, which can make a word unrecognisable. The fix: learn the stress first. Two-syllable nouns/adjectives -> 1st; verbs -> 2nd; -tion/-ic/-ity -> penult. Then reduce everything unstressed to schwa.
